SITEMAP CRAWLER · LIVE GOOGLE SHEET · DAILY RECHECK · SEARCH CONSOLE + GA4

AUDITFORGE

The audit that clocks in every morning.

The problem: the SEO sends the audit, then everyone goes quiet. Three weeks later I am asking "so what's actually been fixed?" and getting a shrug. The audit was the deliverable. The fix never was. What I shipped: give it a sitemap and it crawls every URL plus every link, image and script those pages point to, into a Google Sheet that fills up while it runs. 118,008 pages, 480,986 findings, one laptop, one night. Every row has a proof link that opens the live page and scrolls to the exact text. It also writes the correction: the page a broken URL should redirect to, the heading outline a page should have ("Change H4 → H3"), rolled into one fix list, page by page, biggest Google traffic first. The part I am proud of: it does not stop at the report. Every morning at 6 it rechecks every finding on the live site, done or still open, and emails me. Delete a row from the sheet and that finding stops being chased: the team's edits are the spec. Search Console and GA4 are wired in, so every fixed page is measured before vs after against pages not fixed yet. A control group, not a dashboard. At 14, 30 and 90 days an impact report emails itself. What it taught me: LinkedIn answers crawlers with 404 while the page works fine; a tool that calls that broken is lying to you. And the hardest bugs were Windows file handles and Wi-Fi dropping at 4am. Production is the part after the demo.

02
LLM WRITER · FACT GUARD · APPROVAL GATE · COST CAP · SMARTLEAD REPORTING

CADENCE

Four emails per prospect. Zero that a machine let through unchecked.

The problem: sales researched every prospect by hand and wrote four emails each. An hour per lead, quality falling off a cliff after lunch, and the occasional email congratulating someone on a funding round that belonged to a different company. What I shipped: a sheet of company names goes in; a finished four-touch cadence comes out. It finds a real news trigger from this quarter, frames the decision the prospect is actually facing, and writes to that. Then the part that earns its keep: every email passes a rules check, a fact guard that ignores our own framing and flags only real claims, a sampled second judge, and an approval gate that is the only thing allowed to mark a row Ready. A bad sentence gets rebuilt and the row re-runs every check; a wrong company, a stale story or a duplicate contact gets held, never repaired. The call I made: a hard cost cap of $0.90 per 1,000 prospects. As the run approaches it, the expensive steps switch off in order of how much they matter. The fact guard never switches off. No row is Ready unchecked, ever. The reporting: a scoreboard that pulls sent, opens, replies and bounces per campaign from Smartlead at 7am daily and redeploys itself. No server, no database, the API key never leaves GitHub Secrets. 7,200 prospects in the last run. Sales woke up to it finished and took the credit. I have made my peace with that.

03
VERIFIED AI OUTPUT · 66 CHECKS · SHEETS API · NEXT.JS

REDLINE

The QA pass that is not allowed to make things up.

The problem: a formatting miss went live after four people read the page and all four read past it. Casing, a double space, a growth rate that did not survive arithmetic. Invisible in a CMS field, embarrassing on a published report. What I shipped: paste a live URL. It runs 66 checklist rows over the parsed page, requests every link and image, recomputes the CAGR instead of reading it, compares the page against the last four published reports, and writes a Yes or No row to the master sheet. The call that matters: three of those rows genuinely need a model, because spelling and tone are not regex problems. So the model is never trusted, only verified. It has to quote the exact span it objects to, the server confirms that span appears in the page source character for character, and the fix must be a minimal edit of what it quoted. An early build passed a finding that quoted a real sentence and invented a typo inside it, so now anything failing a guard is discarded, counted, and shown in the run summary. Drift is visible instead of silent. With no API key set, those rows read "Not run" rather than guessing. Live and in use.

04
SOURCE-LEVEL TEARDOWN · 13 WEIGHTED FACTORS · SEARCH CONSOLE OAUTH · HREFLANG REPORT

X-RAY

Turns "why are we losing?" into a ranked to-do list.

The problem: "why does that page outrank ours" was being answered with instinct and the phrase "content is king", which is not a roadmap. What I shipped: point it at a URL. It finds the page each of four rival research firms ranks with for the same intent, tears both down at source level, and scores the gap across 13 weighted factors: content depth, schema, in-content links, authorship and E-E-A-T, outbound citations, FAQ and AI-Overview coverage, freshness, down to image alt text. Lists every link inside the report body verbatim, with the chrome links counted separately, because a rival with 400 menu links is not beating you on internal linking. The call I made: every factor carries an explicit weight, and that is the whole product. A weight can be argued with in a meeting and tuned when it is wrong. A gut feeling can only be agreed with or ignored. So the output is not a diagnosis, it is a prioritized backlog, biggest scoring gap first. The extras nobody asked for until they needed them: Search Console OAuth adds live rank, CTR, impressions and clicks to every row; bulk mode runs a whole CSV; a weekly hreflang report shows clicks per language sitemap per week, so "is Japan growing?" is a filter, not a project. Rival sites answer crawlers with 403; those get marked blocked, not failed, and the rest of the verdict still lands.

05
NEXT.JS · SEARCH CONSOLE + GA4 · URL INSPECTION API · NO DATABASE

PULSE

Every property, one screen, refreshed before anyone asks.

The problem: "how are we doing?" was answered with six browser tabs, a screenshot and a number that was already three days old by the time it hit Slack. What I shipped: one read-only dashboard for every Search Console and GA4 property the account can see. Clicks, impressions, CTR and position with the change against the previous period; pages in positions 1–10, 11–20, 21–30; pages that dropped five places or vanished; queries one nudge away from page one; branded share; users on the site right now, by country and city. Every table filters, sorts and exports. It pairs each Search Console site to its GA4 property by domain, and remembers if you correct it. The call I made: Google does not expose the indexing report through its API, so I rebuilt it. Sitemap URLs plus every page with impressions count as indexed for free; the remainder go through the URL Inspection quota of about 2,000 checks a day, resumable, cached in the browser. Slow, honest, and the first time anyone here could see the not-indexed list without clicking through Search Console one property at a time. The detail that bit me: Search Console lags three days, so every range ends three days ago. Otherwise every Monday looks like a traffic crash. Tokens stay server-side, sign-in is an allowlist, and it refreshes itself every five minutes so "any update?" gets answered by a URL.
